# Release Notes: 26 November 2026

## 1. Acts Between Communities, Recusal, and Rules Written in Words

- **Crossings** ([decision 0004](../decisions/0004-crossings-and-votes-borne.md)). An act one community sends to another is entered on both sides. The receiver returns a receipt that the sender verifies against the receiver's head. A notary keeps the heads it has witnessed, and a community that rewrote an entry it had shown cannot have its next head witnessed. A party that leaves takes the acts that concern it, each provable to anyone who knows the head.
- **Votes counted for those who answer.** A vote counts for the party that answers for it, so an envoy who votes under a community's mandate votes for the community.
- **Compacts.** An amendment can ratify a compact, and the constitution keeps the compacts it has ratified.
- **Recusal.** A party to a claim, or anyone of the origin of the party it is against, may not answer it.
- **Institutional statements** ([decision 0005](../decisions/0005-institutional-statements.md)). A rule written in words, such as "steward must answer claim after a claim within 1 round or else the claim stands unanswered on the card.", is read by a grammar into its parts and written back unchanged. A text that is not a rule is refused with where it stops. A constitution's own rules are read the same way, and each duty's regularity is measured from the record.
