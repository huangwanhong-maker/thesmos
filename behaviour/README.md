# behaviour: A Taxonomy of Behaviour

Status: `draft` (decision 0008).

## 1. Concept

Behaviour falls into five categories: normative acts, finite in kind and constituted by their chronicle; conduct, open and continuous, evidenced by the behaviour journal; events; states; and observations. Normative acts are classed by the source of their effect (juristic acts unilateral, bilateral, collective and constitutive; quasi-juristic acts; acts of evidence; expressive acts), and every effect is composed of the ground's effect primitives, a few of which the law produces with no author.

A community extends the society's law in declared forms and no other: a kind declared by counting as a kind in force, keeping its force, mapping its fields and adding conditions, whose effects are its primitive's (Proposition 14); a category of conduct, a region of the space of conduct declared by a predicate in a closed language, read by the fold only when every feature it reads is carried in an act's envelope; a counts-as rule from such a category to a status a norm reads; a trigger rule producing an effect by law; a standard of recognition. The base taxonomy is data (`schema/behaviour.json`); as an ontology of kategoria it is a forest under one root in the registers Manifestational < Relational < Ontological, its revisions admitted when their map of kinds is a functor keeping every kind of the ground, records carried as the left Kan extension (Proposition 17). Since decision 0009 the base taxonomy also states `present` (quasi-juristic, its effects party and mandate) and `recognise` (unilateral juristic, its effect recognition), with the effect primitives party and recognition.

The base in force is the taxonomy's second version (`schema/behaviour-2.json`, decision 0010), a revision of the first (`schema/behaviour.json`, kept as adopted) along which records carry. It describes one occurrence at five levels: E epistemic behaviour, O ontological conduct, M manifest conduct, S social conduct (indexed by the structure that attributes it) and N normative conduct (indexed by the order, in two forms: constituted acts, the first version's normative acts, and qualified conduct), events and states kept beside them. Six facets cross the levels (stage, polarity, agency, modality, intention, scope; the formation of intent epistemic alone), nine bridges join them (enactment, disclosure, manifestation, recognition by evidence, social attribution, codified counts-as, custom formation, authorisation and obligation, feedback), and practices are templates of linked descriptions (a protest, an act through an agent, a review; communities declare more). A description of an occurrence is at one level and of one kind there, an occurrence at as many levels as it has descriptions. A feature declares its level, E or conduct's, and no counts-as rule or declared condition reads epistemic behaviour alone (invariant G10). It adds `agree` and `denounce`, the effect primitive agreement and its end by law, and the features of disclosed epistemic behaviour. The base in force since decision 0011 is the third version (`schema/behaviour-3.json`), a revision of the second that renames nothing: it declares the directives `apply`, `question` and `petition`, kinds counting as `request` of the types application, question and petition, each with its conditions of form. The base in force since decision 0012 is the fourth version (`schema/behaviour-4.json`), a revision of the third that renames nothing: items are an effect primitive and their reopening an effect by law, and the kinds that act on items say so and read the fields they now read.


Since decision 0013 the base in force is the fifth version (`schema/behaviour-5.json`), a revision of the fourth that renames nothing: the author's kind and declared provider, features the registry of parties carries beside the envelope; the category *reading by an operative*; and the counts-as rule by which such a reading counts as a transmission to its provider, applied where a constitution declares that operatives transmit to their provider. A counts-as rule may name the declaration it applies under (`where declared`); the base's are always in force, with the categories and features they read, and inert until the declaration is made.
## 2. Content

In `behaviour.hpp`: `Category`, `Register`, `register_of`; `ident`, `nameable`; predicates (`Atom`, `Predicate`, `read_predicate`, `Features`, `holds`, `implies`, `contained`, `carried`, `fold_readable`, `features_of`); `Episode`, `canonical`, `digest`; the parts of a taxonomy (`Effect`, `Primitive`, `Declared`, `Feature`, `Conduct`, `CountsAs`, `Trigger`, `Standard`, `Part`); `read_part`, `canonical`, `base`, `in_force`, `path`, `primitive_of`; `faults`; `Reduced`, `reduce`; `image`, `revision_faults`; `distance`, `categorise`, `omissions`; `Observation`, `standing`.

In `behaviour.hpp` since decision 0010: `Level`, `Facet`, `Bridge`, `Slot`, `Step`, `Practice`; `first_base`, `second_base`, `third_base`, `fourth_base`, `base` (since decision 0013 the fifth version); `known_declaration` and `features_of` with the registry (decision 0013); `level_read`, `epistemic_only`.

In `ontology.hpp` (on kategoria's `ontologies`): `fixed_kinds`, `ontology_of`, `mapping_of`, `verify`, `admit_revision`, `record_of`, `carry`, `carried_as_extension`; since decision 0010 `level_kind`, `constituted_kind`, `acts_root`, `conduct_root`, `observation_root`, `relation_name`.

In `levels.hpp` (decision 0010): `Description`, `Linked`, `Descriptions`, `kind_of`, `levels_of`, `faults`, `record_of`, `instances`.

The behaviour journal and its join to the chronicle are in `chronicle/conduct.hpp`; the fold's reduction of declared kinds, its trigger rules and its counts-as rules in `chronicle/chronicle.hpp`.
