# The Theory of thesmos

This note states what thesmos computes and what it guarantees. Its source is the design record *A Society of Human and AI Communities* (2026-11-25) in the infrastructure, corrected after an independent reading for error on the same day.

## 1. Parties and Origins

**Definition 1.** A *party* is an identified participant of one declared kind: person, organisation, operative (an identity under which a program acts) or community. Persons, organisations and communities are *answerable*; operatives are not.

**Definition 2.** *Control* is a declared partial function from operatives to parties (joint control is declared as control by one party, an organisation of the controllers); *provision* names an operative's provider and model. A relation of control with an unknown controller or a cycle of operatives is faulty and refused.

**Definition 3.** The *origin* of a party under control is the first party that is not an operative reached along control; an operative whose control is undeclared is its own origin, by declaration. Under provision, the origin of an operative is its provider.

*Remark.* Sameness of origin is sameness under a named criterion. Two operatives whose traces coincide stay distinct until a declared relation joins them.

## 2. Mandates

**Definition 4.** A *mandate* states a principal, a holder, kinds of act, matters, an allowance and a slice for each resource in that resource's own unit, a term in turns, whether it may be delegated, and its parent. A chain *stands* when (i) its root principal is answerable, (ii) each link was issued by the holder of its parent and the parent may be delegated, or is a split of its parent (Definition 20), (iii) each link narrows its parent in kinds, matters, allowance and term, and in the communities and the term in instants of Definition 19, and (iv) no link is revoked.

*Remark.* Clause (ii) gained its second case with passports ([decision 0007](../development/decisions/0007-passports.md)); a chain without splits stands exactly when it stood before.

**Proposition 2.** In a standing chain every link's scope lies within every link above it. If each charge is made against every link from the charged one to the root, the use beneath any link is at most its ceiling.

*Proof.* Inclusion and order are transitive; a charge is admitted only when every link above has room and is then added to each. ∎ With ceilings charged apart the bound fails: two children of 80 under a root of 100 admit 160.

**Proposition 3.** Let one verifier, remote from the place where a mandate's status is kept, check the status at fixed windows and admit at most σ of a resource between two checks; let a revocation be known at the place of keeping when it is entered there. Then what the verifier admits after a revocation is at most min(B, σ), B the allowance remaining.

*Proof.* The verifier learns of the revocation at its next check; before it lies part of one window, in which at most σ, and at most what remains, is admitted. ∎ The community's own fold keeps the status and knows a revocation at once.

## 3. Acts and the Chronicle

**Definition 5.** An *act* has an author, a kind of the ground with its force (expressive, assertive, directive, commissive, declarative), a turn, an optional mandate, a typed envelope of fields, and a free body.

**Definition 6.** A community's *chronicle* is the sequence of the acts it admitted, each sealed as archeion seals a journal's entries: the envelope's canonical text as what the entry is about, the body as its note. The *fold* `step` advances the relational state (positions, mandates, revocations, commitments, grants, claims, proposals, assessments, charges, rules in force) by one act, or refuses it and changes nothing.

**Proposition 1.** Given the declared parties with their relation of control, the constitution in force at the founding and a chronicle that seals, the relational state is determined. Two chronicles that differ only in bodies give the same relational state and different seals.

*Proof.* `step` is a function of the state and of the envelope; induction on the chronicle. A body enters the seal and stays out of `step`. ∎

## 4. Commitments, Set-off and Novation

**Definition 7.** A commitment is conditional (an offer), active, presented, handed over (the performer's authority ended and what remains is stated), discharged, released, cancelled (a breach) or withdrawn; overdue is a mark on an active commitment past its due turn. Its debtor is the party that answers for the act that made it.

**Proposition 5.** Let o(i, j) be whole-number obligations of one class in which set-off is agreed, and N the sum of the positive net obligations. (1) Cancelling a common amount around a cycle keeps every net obligation. (2) Cancelling cycles to exhaustion leaves an acyclic residual. (3) Its volume is at least N. (4) On it, replacing "i owes j, j owes k" by "i owes k" with the consent of the three, as far as it goes, ends with volume exactly N and every party purely a debtor or purely a creditor, net obligations kept.

*Proof.* (1) A cycle enters and leaves each of its parties once. (2) By construction. (3) Each party owes at least its positive net obligation. (4) Each replacement lowers the volume by its amount, at least one unit, so the process ends; it keeps net obligations as (1) does and adds an edge only along an existing path, so the residual stays acyclic; at the end no party both owes and is owed, so each debtor owes exactly its net obligation. ∎ Rational amounts are scaled to whole numbers. Cancelling cycles in some order can clear less than the greatest circulation, a problem of minimum-cost flow.

## 5. Counts and Rules

**Definition 8.** A rule of aggregation declares its unit of count. By operative, each voter counts one. By origin, each origin weighs one, divided equally among its voters. By person or by community, the same over origins of that kind alone.

**Proposition 6 (as corrected).** (a) Every origin weighs one whatever the number of its voters. (b) When an origin whose voters vote alike replaces them by any number of voters that vote as they did, the share in favour is unchanged. (c) When an origin's voters divide, adding voters to it moves its own weight in favour within [0, 1], and so moves the share by at most 1/O, O the number of origins counted.

*Proof.* The share is the mean over origins of each origin's share of voters in favour; an origin's term lies in [0, 1] and is unchanged when its voters vote alike. ∎ The statement first written in the design omitted the condition of (b); a divided origin that adds a voter in favour moves the share from 3/4 to 5/6, within the bound of (c).

## 6. Constitutions

**Definition 9.** A *constitution* declares positions with the kinds of party that may hold them, boundary rules of entry and exit, choice rules (who may, must and must never make which kinds of act, in which scenes), bodies, decisions with a body, a rule, a unit of count and a tempo, scenes, conversions between registers, receiving capacity, forms of circulation, a route for claims, the custody of means with any reserve, preserved properties, a rule of amendment for each level with what is entrenched, a policy of records, and tempi.

**Admission.** A constitution is admitted when nine conditions of form hold: closure; decisions with bodies, rules and units, and bodies that can be filled; amendment at each level and entrenchment named; a route for claims; conversions listed; custody declared, with every executable power recognised or reserved with scope and term; preserved properties declared; receiving capacity and forms of circulation; a policy of records. The fold refuses an amendment whose result would not be admitted.

## 7. The Schema of the Ground

The ground's rules of answerability are stated as equations of a category (`schema/ground.cat`): a root is its own root; the party that answers for a chain is its root's principal and is answerable; an act under a mandate is made by its holder and answered for by its root's principal; a commitment's debtor is the party that answers for the act that made it; a crossing act is sent from the sender's chronicle and entered in the receiver's. A record is a set-valued functor on this category; kategoria answers whether a record is one, and finds a record that breaks a rule by the equation it breaks.

## 8. The Facets of a Norm

Regularity (the share of applicable episodes that conform) and operation (the decisions that invoke the norm) are measured from a chronicle; adoption and justification are acts. A norm invoked and never adopted is marked *operative, unadopted*; one adopted and never invoked *adopted, inoperative*.

## 9. Holdings and the Norms of Access

A community holds resources of many kinds, and declares norms that say who may reach them and how ([decision 0006](../development/decisions/0006-holdings-and-the-norms-of-access.md)). The norms are written in the grammar of institutions (Crawford and Ostrom 1995; Frantz and Siddiki 2021), and their resolution is declared, so that every request has one answer and the answer names its ground.

**Definition 10 (holding).** A *holding* h has an identifier, a kind (an open vocabulary: data, document, tool, budget, capacity, knowledge, briefing, the time of agents, a reference to an account kept elsewhere), a custodian (an office of the constitution or a named party), a provenance (a SHA-256 digest by archeion's rule, a source, a licence), possibly a container c(h), and, when metered, a unit u(h) and a quantity q(h) ∈ ℕ. The containers form a forest. What is deposited within a holding that declares review is *pending* until a party of another origin than its depositor's attests it. A holding has a *visibility*: public, members' (the default: anyone who holds a position of the community) or that of named offices. Visibility says who may know of the holding and read its description without an act; reading its content is an act of access, decided by the norms.

**Definition 11 (norm of access, request).** A *norm of access* is n = (A, D, a, O, C, E):

- an attribute A: anyone; a kind of party; anyone who holds a position; a party whose origin holds the position p; the position p; the role custodian or depositor of the holding;
- a deontic D ∈ {may, must, must not};
- an aim a ∈ {consult, use, deposit, modify, lend, withdraw};
- an object O: any holding; the kind k; the holding g with what is kept within it;
- conditions C, a conjunction of: under a mandate covering the kinds of act K and the matters M; within a quota Q a round for the party that answers for the act; by need under a standard S, attested in the record; for a purpose in P; a *transmission*, the services outside the community to which what is reached may be sent (a set T, or any);
- an or-else E.

A *request* r = (σ, t, a, x, π) has a situation σ (what the record says of the party: the positions it holds, those held by the party that answers for its act, those of its origin, the mandate it acts under, the standards under which its need is attested by the last assessment of it under each criterion made by a party of another origin, and what the party that answers drew under each norm in the turn), a target t (a holding, or the holding a deposit would make), an aim a, an amount x, a purpose π and the service τ outside the community to which the act declares it sends what it reaches (none, if it declares none). A permission's transmission condition holds when τ is none or lies in T; a permission silent on transmission permits none. A prohibition's transmission condition holds when τ lies in T; a prohibition silent on transmission forbids whatever is sent. A norm *matches* r when its aim is a, its attribute holds of σ and t, and its object covers t (any holding; t of kind k; g is t, or one of t's containers).

**Definition 12 (specificity).** For a norm n that matches r, s(n, r) = (ω, −d, α) ∈ ℤ³, ordered lexicographically, where ω is 2, 1 or 0 as O names a holding, a kind or any holding; d is the number of steps from t to the holding O names (0 for the other objects); and α is 6 for a role, 5 for a position the author holds itself, 4 for a position held only by the party that answers for its act, 3 for an origin, 2 for anyone who holds a position, 1 for a kind of party and 0 for anyone.

*Remark.* The object comes first, as archeion's access rules put a rule on a unit before one on a class and the nearer unit first. Among attributes a role towards the very holding is the most specific; a position the author holds itself outranks one that reaches it through its mandate's root principal, so that a cooperative may bind its members' hands more narrowly than its members.

**Definition 13 (decision).** Let M(r) be the norms that match r. If M is empty, r is permitted exactly when the constitution declares the kind of t open to a. Otherwise let k be the greatest specificity over M, M\* = {n ∈ M : s(n, r) = k}, and A = {n ∈ M\* : C\_n holds of r}. Then r is refused when A holds a prohibition; permitted when A holds a permission (may or must) and no prohibition; and refused otherwise, the conditions failed named. The norm cited is the first in declared order among the prohibitions of A; failing those, the permissions of A; failing those, the permissions of M\*; failing those, M\*.

**Proposition 8 (determinism of the decision).** (a) The verdict is a function of the set of norms, the request and the record; the declared order of the norms chooses only the norm cited. (b) A request is permitted only by a norm of greatest specificity among those that match, whose conditions hold, while no prohibition of equal specificity has its conditions hold. (c) A norm added below the deciding specificity leaves the verdict as it was, and a prohibition without conditions added at it refuses.

*Proof.* The lexicographic order on ℤ³ is total, so a finite non-empty M has a greatest specificity k and M\* is defined. M, M\* and A are defined by comprehension over the set of norms, and the verdict is [A ∩ F = ∅ ∧ A ∩ P ≠ ∅], with F the prohibitions and P the permissions, a condition on sets; the declared order enters only the choice of the norm cited. (b) is Definition 13 read off. (c) A norm of lower specificity changes neither k nor M\*; a prohibition without conditions at specificity k enters A. ∎

**Definition 14 (access in the fold).** An act of kind consult, use, deposit, modify, lend or withdraw names a holding. It is admitted when the holding allows it (a deposit of a new holding names its kind and, when unmetered, its digest; a draw names an amount no greater than what is available, the quantity less what is lent and not yet due back; a pending holding is consulted and checked, not used or lent; a modification names a new digest and keeps the old among the versions; a withdrawal of an unmetered holding leaves a marker) and the norms of access permit it under Definition 13. An access admitted is recorded with its author, the party that answers for it, the holding, the aim, the amount, the purpose, the service outside the community it sent what it reached to, if any, and the norm that permitted it. A draw from a metered holding made under a mandate is charged along the mandate's whole chain in that holding's own unit (invariant G4 of the design).

**Proposition 9 (charges bounded along chains).** At every prefix of a chronicle: (a) a metered holding's quantity is its declared quantity, plus what was deposited, less what was used and withdrawn, and is never negative, nor is what is available; (b) beneath every link of every chain of mandates, what is charged for a holding is at most that link's ceiling for it and equals the sum of the draws made under mandates whose chain holds the link; (c) what the parties answering to one party drew in one turn under a norm with quota Q is at most Q.

*Proof.* (a) By induction on the admitted acts: a deposit adds its amount; a use or a withdrawal removes x no greater than what is available, which is no greater than the quantity; a loan leaves the quantity and holds x out of what is available until its term. (b) Proposition 2, the draw being one more charge made against every link to the root and admitted only when each has room; nothing else is charged for the holding. (c) The situation counts, from the record, what the party that answers drew under the norm in the turn, and the condition admits x only when that count and x together are at most Q; the count is kept by the party that answers, so its operatives share it. ∎ A loan is charged when it is made, and what it held returns to what is available after its term.

**Definition 15 (briefing).** The *briefing* of a party, acting in its own name or under a mandate, gives the community's mission (its constitution's purpose); the positions it holds and those of the party that answers for it; the kinds of act the choice rules (and its mandate) leave open to it; for each holding it may know of (by its visibility) and each new holding a deposit would make, every aim on which a norm speaks to it or which open access opens, with the decision, the norm and, under a quota, what remains, marked when its mandate does not cover the act; the duties the norms of access lay on it; and the open work the record shows: deposits awaiting a check it may give, offers it may take, commitments it owes, deliveries awaiting its acceptance, claims it may answer, proposals it may vote on. The context of the work is in the record, never in an agent; a leader is an office, briefed from the same record as anyone.

**Proposition 10 (the briefing equals enumeration).** Over the holdings a party may know of, the briefing computed by one sweep over the norms (each norm, in declared order, against each target and aim it matches, keeping for each pair the norms of greatest specificity met so far) lists a target and aim exactly when some norm matches it or its kind is open to the aim, and lists it with the verdict, the norm cited and the reason that decide gives.

*Proof.* Each matching norm meets the pair once; a norm of greater specificity replaces what was kept and one of equal specificity is appended, so at the end the pair keeps its greatest specificity and the norms attaining it in declared order, which are M\* in declared order. The resolution on M\* is the function decide applies to M\*. A pair that no norm matches is listed exactly when open access applies, with decide's open verdict. ∎

**Definition 16 (profile).** A constitution's policy of records may declare the visibility of each of its parts and of two parts of the community's state, who holds which position and the work posted; a part not declared is public. The purpose, the boundary rules and the route for claims are always public: an outsider reads them to judge whether to join and how to claim (G8), and a policy that keeps one of them from the public is refused. The *profile* of a community for a reader gives its mission, its admission policy and its route for claims, and each part shown to the reader: its constitution's parts, its offices and their holders, the work posted (requests, and offers awaiting taking), the holdings whose visibility the reader meets and the norms of access. The *public profile*, read with no reader, is what a directory of communities lists. The ground fixes no policy of what is public; each community declares its own.

**Admission.** The form of what a constitution declares of its holdings is held within two of the nine conditions: closure (condition 1) asks that every norm has an identifier of its own, one deontic and an aim, binds a declared position, kind of party or role, and names a declared holding or kind; the custody of means (condition 6) asks that every holding has a kind and a custodian that is a declared office or a named party (a party custodian is checked against the community's parties at the founding), a metered holding a quantity no less than nothing, an unmetered one none, containers that are declared and acyclic, a review the ground knows, and a provenance digest by archeion's rule where one is given; closure also asks that a holding and a part of the constitution be shown only to declared offices, and that the policy of records names only parts there are; the policy of records (condition 9) and the route for claims (condition 4) ask that the purpose, the boundary rules and the route for claims stay public. A constitution that declares no holdings is admitted as before.

## 10. Passports

An agent that leaves its home for another community carries a document of what it may do there and who answers for it ([decision 0007](../development/decisions/0007-passports.md)). The document is issued by the home, from the home's record, and verified by anyone from the document alone and a head it knows. thesmos keeps no registry of identities: the home that issues a passport is the principal's own community, for a person who belongs to no other a household of one person and their agents.

**Definition 17 (home).** A party's *home* is a community in whose chronicle a mandate of which it is the root principal is an act. A party may have several homes, and each issues only the mandates its own rules let it issue.

**Definition 18 (household).** A *household* is a community whose constitution gives the position principal to persons alone, entered only at its founding, gives the position agent to operatives alone, and empowers the principal alone to issue and revoke mandates. It is the smallest home: it lets a person send agents without belonging to any other community. thesmos fixes no household; a constitution of this form is one, and the infrastructure's communities domain keeps one as a preset.

**Definition 19 (the parts of a mandate that passports read).** A mandate m may state a set C(m) of communities in which it may be used beyond its home (none named when empty), the SHA-256 digest κ(m) of its holder's bearer credential, a reservation ρ(m) ∈ {0, 1}, and a term in instants [σ(m), τ(m)], each bound an instant of ISO 8601 in UTC or absent. A child c *narrows* its parent p in these parts when C(p) = ∅ or ∅ ≠ C(c) ⊆ C(p), and σ(c) ≥ σ(p) wherever σ(p) is stated, τ(c) ≤ τ(p) wherever τ(p) is stated. An act made at an instant outside its mandate's term in instants is refused. A part left unstated is written into no envelope, so a mandate that states none of them has the canonical text it had before.

*Remark.* Narrowing reads C(p) = ∅ as unrestricted, so that a root may name no host and its parts name one each; presentation at a host, the next phase's act, reads C(m) = ∅ as nowhere outside the home, so that a mandate is shown only where it names the host.

**Definition 20 (split, reservation, charged links).** A child c of p is a *split* of p when ρ(c) = 1, c has p's holder, and c is issued by p's principal. For a mandate m whose chain is l₀, …, l_k = m, let j be the greatest index with ρ(l_j) = 1, or 0 when none reserves; the *charged links* of m are L(m) = (l_j, …, l_k). When a reserving mandate m is issued, its ceiling a_m(r) for each resource r is charged against every link of L(parent(m)); a charge made later under a mandate m′ is charged against every link of L(m′). The fold admits the issue or the charge only when every link charged has room for everything the act charges to it.

*Rationale.* A principal who sends one operative to several hosts issues, under the operative's root, one split for each host (the design's Definition 39). Each host sees only its own charges, so independent ceilings could together exceed the root's (Proposition 2's counterexample, with hosts as the children). A split reserves its ceiling at the root when it is issued, so the parts never pass the whole; what is later charged beneath it is charged from it down, since the links above already hold its reservation.

**Definition 21 (witness, passport, verification).** A *witness* of a head h = (H, n, R) (a community, a number of entries, a root) by a notary N is an attestation act of a party of N whose envelope names H, n and R, entered in N's chronicle, with its seal and its proof of inclusion under N's head h_N. A *passport* issued by a home H for a mandate m is: H's head h; for each link l_i of m's chain, root first, the act A_i of kind mandate that issued it (its body left out), its seal s_i and a proof π_i of inclusion of s_i ‖ e(A_i) under h, e the canonical text of an envelope; witnesses of h; the root principal's identifier and kind; and the holder's, with, for an operative, its declared controller, provider and model. A verifier *trusts* a set of homes' heads and a set of notaries' heads, and the passport is *shown* at an instant t, at a host x and with a credential c, each of which may be left out. The passport *holds* when (a) every A_i is an act of kind mandate made by its grant's principal, l₀ is a root and each l_i is issued under l_{i−1}; (b) the chain stands (Definition 4) with the named principal as its root's principal; (c) the named holder holds m, and an operative holder's controller is the principal of some l_i; (d) every π_i verifies s_i ‖ e(A_i) under h, at distinct places of a tree of n leaves; (e) h is a trusted home's head, or some witness of h verifies and its notary's head is trusted; and, as shown, (f) σ(m) ≤ t ≤ τ(m) where stated, (g) x ∈ C(m), (h) SHA-256(c) = κ(m).

**Proposition 11 (a passport is the record of its issue).** (a) Let a home's state S admit the acts issuing the chain of m, let m stand in S, state κ(m), and, if its holder is an operative, let its declared controller have issued a link of the chain. Then issue(H, S, m) gives a passport that holds for a verifier who trusts S's head, shown at an instant within m's term in instants, at a host m names, with a credential whose digest is κ(m). (b) If a passport holds for a verifier whose trusted heads are heads of the chronicles they name, then every A_i, with exactly the envelope the passport writes, is an entry of H's chronicle among its first n, with seal s_i; so every part of every link that its envelope seals (principal, holder, kinds, matters, allowance, slice, the terms, delegability, parent, communities, credential, reservation) is as the home entered it, unless a collision of SHA-256 is exhibited. (c) An alteration of a sealed part of a link, of a seal, of a proof or of the head makes the passport fail, unless the altered head is itself trusted or a collision is exhibited.

*Proof.* (a) issue takes each A_i from S's admitted acts, with its seal, and proves its leaf under S's head by RFC 6962's construction, so (d) holds, and (e) holds since S's head is trusted. The chain stands in S, and the faults of Definition 4 are a function of the links and of the root principal's kind, which the passport carries, so (b) holds again; (a) and (c) hold since the passport's chain is S's chain of m and the controller was checked at issue; (f) to (h) hold by hypothesis. (b) A proof of inclusion that verifies from a datum to the root R of a tree of n leaves, leaf and inner nodes hashed apart (RFC 6962, §2.1), shows the datum to be a leaf of that tree unless a collision of SHA-256 is exhibited. The datum is s_i ‖ e(A_i), and the canonical text of an envelope is equal for two envelopes exactly when they are equal (Definition 6), so the act is the act entered. Through a witness, the attestation rebuilt from h verifies under the trusted h_N, so N's chronicle holds an attestation of exactly h, and the argument applies to h. (c) An alteration of a sealed part changes e(A_i), one of a seal changes the datum, one of a proof or of n changes the path or the shape of the tree, and each changes the root recomputed in (d); an altered head fails (e) unless it is trusted. ∎

The checks issue passports on 300 random homes (chains of one link and of two, splits and delegations, terms and communities stated or not), each holding, and alter each in thirteen ways, every alteration refused.

*Caveat.* A passport proves its issue and nothing later: a revocation entered after h leaves it holding. Whether a mandate still stands is its status at the home, which a remote verifier learns at its next check (Proposition 3); the next phase enters such reports as acts. Whose heads a verifier trusts is the verifier's to decide.

**Proposition 12 (reservations bound the hosts).** At every prefix of a home's chronicle, for every mandate l and resource r, what is charged to l for r (the ceilings of the reserving mandates m with l ∈ L(parent(m)), and the charges under the mandates m′ with l ∈ L(m′)) is at most l's ceiling a_l(r). Consequently, if c₁, …, c_q are reserving splits of a root R, then Σ_i a_{c_i}(r) ≤ a_R(r); and if each c_i names one host, and each host admits under c_i no more than a_{c_i}(r), then the hosts together admit no more than a_R(r). Without reservation the bound fails: two children of 80 under a root of 100, each charged at its own host, admit 160.

*Proof.* By induction on the admitted acts. Only two kinds of act raise what is charged to l: the issue of a reserving m with l ∈ L(parent(m)), admitted only when l has room for a_m(r) beside everything else the act charges to it, and a charge under m′ with l ∈ L(m′), admitted under the same condition; nothing lowers it, and a refused act changes nothing. A root cannot reserve (it has no parent to reserve against, a fault of its chain), so L(R) = (R) and each split's ceiling is charged to R, whence the sum. A charge under c_i is charged against L(c_i), which begins at c_i, so nothing is charged to R twice. Each host's charges are bounded by c_i's ceiling (Proposition 2 at the host), and the sum of those ceilings is bounded by a_R(r). ∎

The checks issue random splits under random roots in 200 homes, never passing the root's ceiling and recording exactly what they reserve, and let a host for each split charge at random, never passing the root's ceiling together.

*Remark.* A reservation stays charged when its mandate is revoked: a host may admit up to a slice before it learns of the revocation (Proposition 3), and releasing what a revoked part left unspent waits for the host's report of its use.

**Proposition 13 (records made before passports).** A mandate that states none of the parts of Definition 19 is written into its envelope exactly as before; a chain none of whose links reserves is charged along the whole chain; and a mandate with no term in instants is never out of it. So every record made before decision 0007 replays with its seals and its relational state unchanged.

*Proof.* The parts are written only when stated; when no link reserves, j = 0 and L(m) is the whole chain; an unbounded term contains every instant; the other steps of the fold are those of before. ∎ The checks hold a record's seals to those Python's hashlib computes from the envelope as it was written before.

*Remark.* The design numbers these concepts otherwise (its Definitions 36 to 39 and Proposition 17); its numbering continued a theory note that ended at Definition 15, and this note keeps its own sequence.

## 11. A Taxonomy of Behaviour

A society records what its parties do and refers to what happens to it, and law has long kept two kinds of behaviour apart: an act of a kind the law defines, with a form, conditions of validity and effects the law declares; and what a party actually does, to which the law may attach consequences ([decision 0008](../development/decisions/0008-a-taxonomy-of-behaviour.md)). The first are finite in kind, because a society defines them; the second are open, because a new tool or a new model brings new conduct without asking anyone. The record of the first is constitutive, as a resolution exists when it is minuted; the record of the second is evidential, a trace of what occurred. The sources are Austin's felicity conditions, Searle's forces and constitutive rules ("X counts as Y in context C"), Anscombe's and Davidson's actions under a description, the civil-law doctrine of juristic acts (*Rechtsgeschäft*, 民事法律行为) with its quasi-juristic acts and factual acts (*Realakte*, 事实行为), the common law's acts in law and acts in fact, Hohfeld's correlatives, the grammar of institutions, the author's Generative Relational framework with its three registers, and kategoria's ontologies (its decision 0005).

**Definition 22 (behaviour).** *Behaviour* is what a party does, or a society undergoes, that the society records or refers to. It falls into five categories, fixed by the ground: the *normative act*, relational, constituted by its chronicle; *conduct*, ontological, referred to and never established, evidenced by the behaviour journal; the *event*, what happens that is no party's act, ontological; the *state*, what holds at a time, relational when institutional and manifestational when measured; and the *observation*, an assertion about the others, manifestational until it is given standing. The registers are ordered Manifestational < Relational < Ontological, as kybernesis's ontology of facts orders them.

**Definition 23 (normative act, class).** A normative act of kind k in a community is a record with an envelope and a body, admitted by the community's fold. Its kind belongs to the finite set of kinds in force: the ground's and those declared (Definition 31). Each has a force, conditions of validity and effects. An act whose conditions fail is void: the fold refuses it and nothing changes (Austin's misfire). An admitted act whose body is false or insincere is valid and defective (Austin's abuse), a matter of evidence and of claims. The kinds of the ground are classed by the source of their effect: a juristic act, unilateral, bilateral, collective or constitutive, takes its effect from declared will; a quasi-juristic act takes it from the law, whatever was willed; an act of evidence adds evidence under its author's name; an expressive act has none.

**Definition 24 (effect primitive, effect by law).** Every effect of every kind is composed of the ground's *effect primitives*: positions entered and left, mandates issued and revoked, commitments created and moved, grants and charges, holdings reached and deposits checked, claims opened and answered, assessments recorded, proposals voted, adopted and applied, evidence recorded, since decision 0009 parties declared and recognitions set (§12), and since decision 0010 disclosure agreements ratified and denounced, ended by law at their term or notice (§13). An *effect by law* is a form in which the ground lets a primitive change with no author: a position's end at a term, a commitment's lapse past its due and its overdue mark, and a loan's return at its term. The base taxonomy (`schema/behaviour.json`) states each kind of the ground with its class, force, effect primitives and the fields the fold reads.

**Definition 25 (episode, behaviour journal).** An *episode* of conduct is a record of the behaviour journal: the party, the party that answers for it, its provider and model, the community and node, its turn and instants, its operation, the resources it spent in their units, the digests of what it read and wrote, the holdings and items it touched, the kind of the act it constituted, and declared features; the next entry names its outcome. The journal is sealed as archeion seals any.

**Definition 26 (space of conduct, nearness, trajectory).** The *space of conduct* is a product of declared features, each categorical (with an open or a closed vocabulary), ordinal, real (with a unit and a scale), a set of digests or identifiers, or an embedding kept for analysis. Each feature f has a distance d_f in [0, 1]: 0 or 1 on a categorical feature, |rank − rank′|/(n − 1) on an ordinal one, min(1, |a − b|/scale) on a real one, Jaccard's distance on a set; the space has d = Σ w_f d_f, or d = max_f d_f, as declared. A party's *trajectory* is the time-ordered path of its episodes.

**Definition 27 (category of conduct).** A *category of conduct* is a region of the space, declared by a predicate in a closed language: a conjunction of atoms, each a feature's presence, its equality with or membership among quoted values, a set's containing a value, a comparison of a number with a constant, or the author's holding a position, each atom perhaps negated. A category is *decidable* when its predicate reads only recorded, typed features, and *carried* (readable by the fold) when every feature it reads is carried in an act's envelope (its author, kind, turn, instant, mandate and fields). A category defined by a model is *analytic*: it yields observations of grade C, read in analysis, and never decides.

*Rationale.* What decides must be decidable from the record by anyone who holds it, or the state would stop being a function of the record (Proposition 16); and what the fold decides must be decidable from the chronicle alone, since the fold never reads the journal.

**Definition 28 (omission).** An *omission* of a party with respect to a duty is a window of its trajectory, opened by an episode of the duty's opening category and closed after the duty's term, in which no episode of the category the duty requires occurs. An absence of conduct is a party's omission only through a duty that binds it.

**Definition 29 (event, state).** An *event* changes institutional states only through trigger rules (Definition 34); the passage of time is read from turns, and other events enter as observations. An institutional *state* is the fold's relational state; a measured state enters as an observation.

**Definition 30 (observation, grade, standing).** An *observation* states its author and the author's origin, its object, its grade (A a structured record with digests; B a text with its passage; C an account from memory or an analytic category) and what it says. It is a trace while it is a journal entry, and becomes a normative act of evidence when its author enters it in a chronicle as a report, an attestation or an assessment.

**Definition 31 (declared normative kind, reduct).** A community declares a kind k′ by its name, its force, the kind k it counts as (of the ground, or declared), the fields of k it renames from fields of its own and those it fixes, conditions of its own in the closed language reading only what an act carries, and the scenes in which it may be made. The declaration is admitted when k′ reaches a kind of the ground by counting as, with no cycle, its force is the force of that kind, its name is its own, and its conditions read. The *reduct* of an act of k′ is the act with its kind replaced along the chain down to the kind of the ground and each declared kind's fields renamed and fixed in turn, the rest of its envelope (author, turn, instant, mandate, charges, other fields) kept. An act of k′ is admitted when its author's mandate, if any, covers k′ or a kind along its chain, the choice rules permit the reduct and, where they name a declared kind of the chain, that kind, each declared kind's conditions hold of the act as it reads it, and the fold admits the reduct; its effect is the reduct's. The chronicle keeps the act as written, under its declared kind, and its reduct as its institutional reading: one act under two descriptions.

**Proposition 14 (declared kinds add no effect).** Let a community declare kinds by counting as. (a) Every admitted act of a declared kind has exactly the effect of its reduct, which the fold would admit in the same state. (b) If no mandate names a declared kind, the chronicle of the reducts of the admitted acts is admitted act by act under the same constitution and reaches, at every prefix, the same relational state. (c) Answerability and charges are those of the reduct: the same author, mandate, party that answers and charges.

*Proof.* (a) The fold admits an act of a declared kind only when the reduct passes authority (the mandate covering a kind on the chain, which for a reduct under a mandate naming no declared kind is its primitive), the choice rules and the effect's own conditions, and applies the reduct's effect; the declared kind's conditions and permissions are further conditions and add no effect. (b) By induction on the chronicle: at a prefix where the two states agree, an admitted declared act's reduct passes the same checks in the same state, since none of them reads the act's declared kind, and its effect is the same; a primitive act is its own reduct; the settlement of trigger rules reads the state, the rules and the turn, which agree. (c) The reduct keeps the author, mandate and charges, and the party that answers is a function of the author and the mandate. ∎

*Remark.* A mandate that names a declared kind and not its primitive authorises the declared kind alone: its acts are among those a mandate naming the primitive would authorise, so it narrows authority and adds none. The checks hold (a) and (b) on 200 random taxonomies (chains of declared kinds over six kinds of the ground, renamed and fixed fields, conditions) each with 40 random acts: every admitted act's reduct is admitted in the chronicle of reducts and the two reach one relational state.

**Definition 32 (the basic counts-as, the join).** The ground's own constitutive rule: an episode that is a request carrying a well-formed envelope counts as a normative act of the envelope's kind when the fold admits it. The act carries in its typed field `episode` the seal of that episode's entry in the journal, a SHA-256 digest that chains every entry before it and so names that entry alone, and the journal's next entry names the act's seal, or the reason it was refused, or that the episode constituted none.

**Proposition 15 (the constitution of acts).** In a community whose acts are made through the basic counts-as, every admitted act is constituted by exactly one episode, which names the act's seal, and the act names that episode's entry; an episode constitutes at most one act; a refused act leaves an episode naming the reason and nothing in the chronicle.

*Proof.* constitute records the episode, submits one act carrying its entry's seal, and records the outcome in the next entry; a refused act changes neither the state nor the chronicle (invariant 5). Two entries share a seal only if SHA-256 collides, since a seal is the digest of the seal before and the entry's own text, so an episode recorded twice with the same features is two episodes, and each constitutes at most the one act submitted with it. ∎ The checks run 100 random sessions of 30 episodes each, joined in both directions, and find an outcome rewritten to name another seal and an act carrying an episode the journal does not hold.

**Definition 33 (counts-as rule).** A *counts-as rule* "conduct of the category C counts as the status S" has C a decidable category the fold can read and S a status a norm reads; the ground knows one, transmission to a service outside the community, its value a feature the act carries. Where an act declares no transmission and lies in C, the norms of access decide it as sending what it reaches to that service.

**Definition 34 (trigger rule, settlement).** A *trigger rule* "when T, then R" has R an effect by law (Definition 24) and T its form: an active commitment of a matter more than a number of turns past its due lapses, handed over; a position held for more than a number of turns ends. The base's own effects by law (a loan's return, the overdue mark) are applied by the ground where they are read. The fold *settles* a state to a turn by applying the declared rules as functions of the state, the rules and the turn; it settles before judging an act, and keeps the settlement only with an admitted act.

**Definition 35 (standard of recognition).** A *standard* states, for a kind of fact about conduct, the grade an observation must reach and whether its author must be of another origin than the subject's. By the base's rule a fact stands *established* when an observation meets the standard and none meeting it disagrees, *disputed* when observations meeting it disagree, *attested* when an observation of grade B or better exists that does not meet it, and *proposed* otherwise.

**Definition 36 (authorisation and obligation of conduct).** Normative acts bound conduct in three ways: a mandate authorises it within its kinds, matters, allowance and term; a duty obliges conduct of a category within a term, its omission measured (Definition 28); a norm of access permits or forbids conduct on a holding, reading and transmission included.

**Proposition 16 (effects only by act or by rule; invariant G9).** The institutional state of a community at every prefix of its chronicle is a function of the admitted acts, their turns and the rules in force (the constitution with its declared kinds, counts-as and trigger rules). Conduct changes it only through an act it constitutes or an act of evidence that records it.

*Proof.* The fold's step reads the settled state, the act's envelope and the rules in force; settlement reads the state, the rules and the act's turn; counts-as rules read the act's envelope; declared kinds reduce to kinds of the ground (Proposition 14). The journal, the episodes and the bodies enter no step. A refused act's settlement is discarded, so the state after a prefix is the fold of its admitted acts, as a replay computes it. ∎ The checks replay chronicles with the journal withheld, find two different journals behind one record leaving one relational state under different seals, and see a trigger rule lapse a commitment and end a position as a replay does again.

**Definition 37 (the taxonomy as data, a revision).** A *taxonomy* is a document `noetarium.behaviour/1`: the base, or a constitution's part `behaviour` extending it. As an ontology of kategoria it is a forest under one root `behaviour` in the three registers: the normative act and its classes, the kinds of the ground beneath their classes and each declared kind beneath the kind it counts as; conduct and its categories; the event; the state with the measured state; the observation; with the force as a nominal axis required of every normative act and the grade as an ordered axis. A *revision* is a later version stating the version it revises and the kinds and categories it renames. It is admitted when it is admitted by form; every kind of the ground keeps its name; every declared kind has an image that reduces to the same kind of the ground; the map of kinds keeps the order "counts as"; every category has an image whose predicate is shown to contain the old one's, atom by atom; and every feature kept keeps its space, its unit and, if ordinal, its order. A community revises its taxonomy by amendment (`change.behaviour`), decided by the body its constitution names.

*Remark.* On forests, a map of kinds extends to a functor of the orders they generate exactly when it sends each parent arrow to an arrow (kategoria's Proposition 9.4); keeping the kinds of the ground fixed with every declared kind reducing to the same one is kategoria's condition on fixed kinds. The fold decides both directly; `behaviour/ontology.hpp` asks kategoria, and the checks hold the two decisions equal on random revisions.

**Proposition 17 (records survive a revision).** Under an admitted revision: (a) every chronicle replays, each act judged under the taxonomy in force when it was admitted; (b) the revision changes no institutional state but the rules, and every act admitted before keeps its kind of the ground; (c) every episode in an old category lies in the image of that category; and the record of the acts, carried along the map of kinds, is its left Kan extension.

*Proof.* (a) The revision is an amendment, an act of the chronicle; a replay applies it at its place, so each act is judged under the rules then in force (G6). (b) The amendment's effect is a change of the constitution; the fold's other state is untouched; an old declared kind's image reduces to the same kind of the ground, so its reading is kept. (c) Each atom of the image's predicate is implied by an atom of the old one's, every implication being sound on every point of the space (equality implies presence and membership in a superset; a strict or non-strict bound implies every weaker one), so the old region lies within the image's. The last part is kategoria's Proposition 9.10, carried_as_extension deciding it. ∎ The checks admit a revision renaming a kind and widening a category, carry a community's record along it, refuse revisions that move a kind to another kind of the ground, break the order or narrow a category, and see a community adopt a revision by its own procedure, an objection no kind in force after it and its record replaying.

## 12. Presentation and Recognition

An agent crosses into a community as a person crosses a border: it presents a passport its home issued, the host verifies it against what the host's own record knows, and the agent becomes a known party there, holding no position until the host's rules give it one ([decision 0009](../development/decisions/0009-presentation-and-recognition.md)). What a host knows of other communities (their heads, and the level at which it recognises them) is in its own record as acts, so that the host trusts nothing it has not entered.

**Definition 38 (parties declared by acts).** A founding may name its community and declare its parties, each with its kind and, for an operative, its controller and provider; a presentation declares its holder and the root principal of its chain. A party is *declared once*: a declaration of a party unknown adds it, of a party known alike changes nothing, and of a party known with another kind, controller or provider is refused. What an act declares is entered on trial before its author's authority is judged, and withdrawn if the act is refused. A party declared holds no position.

**Definition 39 (recognition).** Recognition is directed: the act `recognise`, made by an office the choice rules empower to make it, sets the level at which this community recognises another, generally or for one matter: none, that the other exists, that its attestations count as evidence, that its envoys' undertakings bind it, that its decisions are honoured, each level including those before it. A community does not recognise itself, and where no choice rule names the kind no one may make it.

**Definition 40 (the office of status, known heads, a foreign link's status).** A constitution's part `passports` names the office of status. A report by that office may state a head of another community it has found, which the host then *knows*, and the status (standing, revoked or suspended) of a mandate of another community's chain presented here, as its home showed it. A report by any other party is evidence and moves nothing.

**Definition 41 (presentation).** The act `present` (assertive; a quasi-juristic act) carries a passport in its field `passport`. Let the host's *trust* be the heads it knows, read as homes' heads, and the heads it knows of communities it recognises at the level of evidence or above, read as notaries' heads (Definition 21). The presentation is admitted when (a) the constitution declares passports; (b) its author is the passport's holder, acting under the passport's own mandate; (c) the passport holds for the host's trust, at the act's instant (which the act must state when the passport's term is in instants) and in the host's own name; (d) the issuer is accepted as the part declares: *witnessed*, the passport holding for the notaries' heads alone; *recognised*, the issuer recognised at the level of evidence; or *named*; (e) the holder declares what the part requires (its provider, its model); (f) no link of the chain stands revoked or suspended as last reported; (g) the principal, and the holder with its control and provision, are declared once (Definition 38), the holder's controller being its principal or a party already known; and (h) each link is unknown here, or known here exactly so as a link of another community. Its effect: the parties declared, each link registered as a mandate of another community with the status "presented", and the passport's digest kept. It gives no position. The credential whose digest the mandate names is shown to whoever receives the act, and is never written in the record, so the fold does not read it.

**Definition 42 (a foreign link's authority and window).** An act under a chain with a link of another community is refused when such a link stands revoked or suspended as last reported. The link's term in turns counts its home's turns and is not read here; its term in instants is, and an act under it states its instant. The *window* of a foreign mandate is what has been charged under it since its status was last reported; a charge is admitted only when the window stays within the mandate's slice.

**Proposition 18 (a presentation rests on the host's own record).** If a presentation is admitted, then every link of its passport is the act its home entered, under a head that the host's office of status reported or that a notary witnessed whom the host recognises at the level of evidence and whose head its office reported, unless a collision of SHA-256 is exhibited; the chain stood at that head, narrows, and names the host; its term held at the act's instant; the issuer is one the host's part `passports` accepts; and the presentation gives its holder no position.

*Proof.* Clause (c) is Proposition 11(b) applied to the trust read from the host's record, whose heads are reported heads (Definition 40); clauses (b), (d) and (e) are checked as stated; the effect of `present` declares parties and registers mandates, and enters no position, which only `found`, `join`, `admit` and `convert` enter. ∎ The checks see a passport refused for want of a known head, refused for want of recognition, admitted once both are in the record, and refused for a forged proof, another host, an expired term, another presenter, another mandate, a community admitting none, an issuer not named, a holder known otherwise; and a witnessed head admitted once its notary is recognised.

**Proposition 19 (a revocation at the home costs a host at most a slice).** Let a host's office of status report a foreign mandate's status from time to time, reporting it revoked from its first report after the home revoked it. Then what the host admits under the mandate at or after the revocation is at most min(B, σ), σ the mandate's slice and B what remained of its allowance.

*Proof.* The charges admitted at or after the revocation and before the next report lie within one window, since a report resets the window; a window holds at most σ (Definition 42) and the allowance at most B (Proposition 2). At the next report the link stands revoked and every act under the chain is refused. ∎ This is Proposition 3 made concrete by the fold. The checks run 300 random homes and hosts (budgets, slices, costs, windows of reports and turns of revocation drawn at random), the host's office reporting the home's actual status: no host loses more than the slice.

**Proposition 20 (hosts and the root, in the fold).** Let an operative's root have reserving splits, each naming one host, each presented at its host and charged there. Then each host's charges under its split are at most the split's ceiling, and all hosts' together at most the root's.

*Proof.* At each host the charged links of a reserving split are the split alone (Definition 20), so its charges are bounded by its ceiling (Proposition 2); the ceilings of the splits are bounded by the root's (Proposition 12). ∎ Without reserving the bound fails: two children of 80 under a root of 100, each presented at its own host, admit 160. The checks run 200 random homes with one or two hosts, and the counterexample.

**Proposition 21 (the state from the record alone).** Let a chronicle declare its parties by acts: its founding its founders, its presentations its agents and their principals. Given the constitution at the founding and a chronicle that seals, the relational state is determined, with no registry of parties beside the record; two chronicles that differ only in their bodies give the same state and different seals.

*Proof.* As Proposition 1: the parties declared are part of the state the fold builds from envelopes (the founding's field `parties`, the presentation's field `passport`), and a refused act's declarations are withdrawn. ∎ The checks run 100 random host chronicles (presentations, recognitions, reports, claims and acts under passports) from an empty registry, each replaying to the same state, and the same chronicles with their bodies rewritten.

*Remark.* The ground's invariants hold of the new acts as follows. G1 (record): a passport's mandate is an act of its home, its presentation an act of the host, each sealed; a recognition and a report of status are acts of the community that makes them. G2 (standing): `present` and the report of status are assertive and give no duty or power; their effects follow conditions that declarative acts laid down (the part `passports`, the recognitions), and a presentation enters no position. G3 (answerability): a presented chain stands, its root a person, an organisation or a community, and a revocation at its home binds the host at its next report within a slice (Proposition 19). G4 (guarded resources): charges under a foreign mandate are made along the chain as the host knows it, in each resource's unit, within its window. G5 (separate registers): a passport is evidence and never a position; recognitions are their own record. G6 (rules as data): the part `passports` is the constitution's, and amended as it is. G7 (exit with the record): the acts a presented agent authored are acts of the host's chronicle, which it takes with it as any party does. G8 (a route for claims): a presentation opens no item and leaves the route as it was. G9 (effects by act or by rule): parties, recognitions, heads and statuses change only by admitted acts.


## 13. Levels of Description and Epistemic Behaviour by Agreement

The first version of the taxonomy cut behaviour into five categories. The author's refinement ([decision 0010](../development/decisions/0010-levels-of-description-and-epistemic-behaviour-by-agreement.md)) reads its highest divisions as *levels of description of one occurrence*, after the doctrine of action under a description (Anscombe, *Intention*, 1957; Davidson, "Agency", 1971): one occurrence may be described as what was intended, what was done, what was recorded, what a community took it to be and what an order qualifies it as. The second version adds the level of epistemic behaviour, which enters a record only by disclosure under an agreement, and the level of social conduct, which a structure attributes.

**Definition 43 (levels of description).** A taxonomy in levels declares five levels, each with a letter, a name and a register: E epistemic behaviour (perceiving, deliberating, planning, forming an intention; Ontological, the subject's interior), O ontological conduct (what is really done; Ontological, referred to and never established), M manifest conduct (traces, records, testimony; Manifestational), S social conduct (conduct as a relational structure takes it; Relational, indexed by the structure) and N normative conduct (conduct defined or qualified by a formal order; Relational, indexed by the order), the last in two forms: *constituted acts*, which exist only within an order and which the chronicle constitutes (the kinds of act beneath their classes), and *qualified conduct*, which exists independently and receives an order's label. Events and states stay beside the levels, occurrences that are no one's. The base's second version is such a taxonomy; a community declares no level, facet or bridge, and may declare practices.

**Definition 44 (description, occurrence, multiple membership).** A *description* is at one level and of one kind there (the level itself, a category of conduct at O, a form or a kind of act at N); a description at S or N names the structure or order that makes it, a description at M has a grade (A, B, C), a qualified conduct carries its label, and a constituted act names its kind. A description *describes an occurrence*; the levels at which an occurrence is described are its *membership*, and an occurrence may be described at several levels at once. Descriptions at S or N by different structures are kept side by side: plurality is never resolved by the record.

**Definition 45 (facets).** A facet is a nominal or ordered set of values that describes the levels it names: the stage (formation of intent < preparation < commencement < execution < completion, after the stage theory of criminal law), polarity (doing, omitting), agency (individual, joint, collective), modality (communicative, physical, computational), intention (accidental < negligent < foreseen < intended) and scope (self-regarding, other-regarding, directed at a structure). The formation of intent is epistemic: a description at E is at that stage when it has one, and a description at O, S or N never is. Attempt and abandonment are qualifications at N of an occurrence whose furthest stage lies below completion.

**Definition 46 (bridges).** A bridge is a declared kind of link from a description at one level to one at another, with the pairs of levels it joins: enactment (E to O), disclosure (E to M), manifestation (O to M), recognition by evidence (M to S or N), social attribution (M to S), codified counts-as (O or S to N), custom formation (S to N), authorisation and obligation (N to E or O), feedback (N to S or E). As an ontology of kategoria each pair a bridge joins is a relation between the two levels' kinds.

**Definition 47 (practices).** A practice is a declared template of slots, each a role at a level and required or optional, with links between roles by bridges whose pairs admit the slots' levels; its required slots are joined by links among themselves. An *instance* of a practice in a record of descriptions fills every required slot with a description at its level, each optional slot where a description at its level is linked as the practice asks, every link the practice asks between filled slots being in the record. The base holds three: a diplomatic protest, an act through an agent, and a review.

**Proposition 22 (the second version is a revision of the first; records carry).** The base's second version is admitted as a revision of the first, by the direct constraints and by kategoria: the map of kinds sends the normative act to the constituted act, conduct to ontological conduct and the observation to manifest conduct, fixes every other root, every class and every kind of the ground, keeps registers and is a functor of the orders the two forests generate; and every record of the first version, carried along it, loses nothing, fits the second, and is the left Kan extension of the record along the map of kinds.

*Proof.* Registers are kept: the normative act and the constituted acts are Relational, conduct and ontological conduct Ontological, the observation and manifest conduct Manifestational, and every other kind keeps its parent's. On forests a map of kinds is a functor exactly when it sends each parent arrow to an arrow (kategoria's Proposition 9.4): a class beneath the normative act goes beneath the constituted act, a category beneath conduct beneath ontological conduct, each root beneath `behaviour` to a kind beneath `behaviour`. The kinds the revision fixes are the first version's but for the three it recasts, and every kind keeps the fixed kind it reduces to: a kind of act keeps its class and itself, a declared kind its kind of the ground, the recast roots and their categories reduce to `behaviour` in both. The force is required beneath the image of the normative act and the grade applies to the image of the observation, so every axis value carried still applies. The last part is kategoria's Proposition 9.10. ∎ The checks admit the revision both ways, refuse one sending conduct to social conduct (its register would change), one renaming an event and one that loses a level, and carry 100 random records of the first version (acts with their force, conduct in its categories, observations with their grade, events and states), each fitting the second and decided the left Kan extension by kategoria.

**Definition 48 (disclosure agreement).** A *disclosure agreement* is a compact between an agent's principal and a community, entered in the community's chronicle and ratified by each side under its own rule: for the principal by an act `agree` it answers for, never subject to the community's choice rules; for the community by an office the choice rules empower to `agree`. Its terms (`noetarium.disclosure/1`) declare its scope (the kinds of epistemic behaviour, reasoning, plan, deliberation or model call; the agents of the principal it covers; the scenes, matters and turns), its purposes (the uses its record admits, among review, good faith, intent, research and dispute, and those it never admits), custody (the holding of kind `epistemic record` that keeps the record, its custodian and its retention), access (the principal, and the positions of a body), cost (who bears it, in which unit, and a ceiling) and withdrawal (who may denounce it, the notice, and whether what was recorded is then kept, returned or erased). It stands *in force* from the turn both sides have ratified the same terms until it *ends*: at its last turn, or when a denunciation's notice has run (an effect by law, the ground's own). From the turn it stands in force the record is held, with norms of access (Definition 11; the attribute *party* binds one party and those that act for it) for the principal and each position of the body: a permission to consult for the purposes admitted and, where some are never admitted, a prohibition for those.

**Definition 49 (epistemic record).** An agent's epistemic episode is recorded in the behaviour journal as level E, an entry `epistemic` naming its party, the party that answers for it, its kind, scene, matter and turn, the agreement and the digest and size of what was disclosed, only when an agreement in force covers it with room under its ceiling; the content is kept in the agreement's record, never in the journal, so that an erasure leaves the journal sealed. Outside an agreement the journal keeps only the entry `reasoning`, its party and its turn. An epistemic entry is joined to the episode of conduct it preceded: the next episode of the same party in the journal. An act that cites an epistemic record uses it, for a purpose its agreement admits and never forbids, after a consult of it for that purpose.

**Definition 50 (a condition that reads epistemic behaviour alone).** An atom of a condition reads the level of the feature it names (a field of an act naming a declared feature reads that feature's level; a feature declaring no level is conduct's, at O; the positions an author holds are read at N). A condition *reads epistemic behaviour alone* when every atom reads a feature of level E. **Invariant G10 (freedom of thought):** no norm attaches an effect to epistemic behaviour alone; it qualifies conduct (intent, good faith, negligence) and never stands for it (*cogitationis poenam nemo patitur*).

**Proposition 23 (freedom of thought).** No taxonomy in force under an admitted constitution, first or by amendment, holds a counts-as rule through a category, or a declared kind with a condition, that reads epistemic behaviour alone; trigger rules read no feature. Hence an effect the rules attach to an act never depends on epistemic features unless conduct or the norms are read with them, and, the fold reading no journal (G9), epistemic entries change no institutional state.

*Proof.* Admission and amendment both judge a taxonomy by its faults, among which is every counts-as rule whose category reads epistemic behaviour alone and every declared condition that does (Definition 50); a trigger rule is one of a closed set of forms over positions and commitments. The second part is Proposition 16. ∎ The checks refuse a counts-as rule and a declared kind reading epistemic behaviour alone, directly and by amendment, admit both when they read conduct too, and on 300 random conditions over epistemic, conduct and normative features find a condition reading epistemic behaviour alone exactly when every atom does, and a counts-as rule through it refused exactly then; and two journals that differ only in their epistemic entries leave one state.

**Proposition 24 (epistemic behaviour enters the record only by agreement).** In a behaviour journal kept through `disclose`, every entry with epistemic content names an agreement in force at its turn that covers it, a digest and a size within the agreement's ceiling as the entries before it stand; every entry outside agreements holds its party and its turn and nothing else; and no entry holds the content itself.

*Proof.* `disclose` writes an entry `epistemic` only for the first agreement in force whose terms cover the episode and whose ceiling has room, with the digest of the content and never the content, and otherwise an entry `reasoning` of two fields. The verification reads each entry back against the chronicle's agreements, which keep the turns of each side's ratification and of a denunciation, so that whether an agreement was in force at a past turn is decided from the record. ∎ The checks record reasoning and model calls under an agreement, and outside it before its ratification, for a kind or an agent or a matter it does not cover, past its ceiling and after its last turn; find a forged entry from a turn when its agreement was not in force and an entry of reasoning that says more than its party and turn; and on 100 random runs of random agreements, ratifications, denunciations and episodes find every disclosure where an independent reckoning of the terms puts it, the verification holding and no content in any entry.

**Proposition 25 (uses of an epistemic record).** A consult of an agreement's record is admitted exactly when the party is the principal (or one that acts for it) and the principal may consult, or holds a position of the body named, for a purpose the terms admit and do not forbid, and the agreement has not ended or, once ended, its record was kept or returned (to the principal alone) and its retention runs; every consult admitted is recorded with its purpose and its norm; an act that cites the record does so only for an admitted purpose after a consult for it.

*Proof.* The norms of Definition 48 are holdings' norms on the record, the most specific deciding, a prohibition prevailing at equal specificity (Definition 13, Proposition 8), so a use the terms never admit is refused whatever else speaks; the end of the agreement is read from its turns before the norms are asked, and nothing but a consult reaches the record. ∎ The checks consult for review and good faith, are refused for intent, for no purpose, by a party the terms do not name, and for any act but a consult; cite the record for good faith after consulting it and are refused for intent and without a consult; see a denunciation take effect after its notice with the record erased, returned or kept to the end of its retention; and on 330 random consults of random agreements find the fold deciding as an independent reading of the terms does.

*Remark.* G1 (record): an agreement is two acts of the chronicle, a denunciation a third, every consult an access recorded; a disclosure is an entry of the sealed journal. G2 (standing): `agree` is commissive and binds the side that makes it; the record's norms follow from the agreement, a declarative arrangement of both sides. G3 (answerability): the principal answers for its agents' disclosures. G4 (guarded resources): what is recorded is counted against the agreement's ceiling, in its unit. G5 (separate registers): epistemic behaviour is level E, a disclosure its manifestation at M, a self-report graded C; neither is conduct or a normative fact. G6 (rules as data): the terms are data, and the taxonomy in levels is revised as any taxonomy is. G7 (exit with the record): the principal takes its agents' record by consulting it within its retention, or by its return. G8 (a route for claims): a dispute is among the purposes an agreement may admit. G9: agreements, records and their uses change only by admitted acts and by the ends their terms declare. G10: Proposition 23.

*Remark.* Withdrawal from compacts in general belongs to the design's Phase 7, where a community ratifies a compact by amendment (`change.compact`, with reservations) and withdraws by amendment (`change.withdraw compact`), effective after the compact's notice, obligations owed staying owed. The disclosure agreement is the first compact whose withdrawal is built: a bilateral compact one of whose parties is not a community, its denunciation by either side effective after a declared notice, what was recorded then disposed of as the terms declare. Phase 7 generalises it in two ways: a compact's community side may ratify and withdraw by amendment under its own decision rule as well as by an empowered office, and a compact of several parties keeps each party's ratification and withdrawal apart, the agreement's two sides being its smallest case.


## 14. Items, Routes and Rulings

A question that has nowhere to go goes to whoever can answer it, and in a society with no centre that is no one, or a person outside the record. Decision 0011 gives every directive a route inside the community: a request that names a type opens an *item* on the route for that type, a body or an office answers it within a term, a party to it never answers it, a ruling becomes precedent, and an application is decided by the boundary rule of the position applied for.

**Definition 51 (item, route).** The ground's directive `request` is generalised: a request that carries a field `type` opens an *item* of that type (an application, a question, a petition, or a type a community declares); a request with no type is as it was. The base taxonomy's third version declares `apply`, `question` and `petition` as kinds that count as `request` of the types application, question and petition, so that each opens its item and adds no effect beyond the request's (Proposition 14). Who may open an item of a type is governed by the choice rules of the declared kind that opens it, not by those of `request`, so that who may post work and who may apply are ruled apart. A *route* names the body that answers an item of its type, its term (a number of turns or a declared tempo), the body that reviews its answers, and for questions the subjects they may be about; a route declared empty reaches no body, and its items are refused with that declaration. A constitution declares its routes in its part `routes`, always public; where it declares none, routes are read from what it declares: questions and a community's declared types go to the route for claims, petitions to its operational amendment, applications to the boundary rule.

**Definition 52 (application).** An application names a position. The position's boundary rule decides it: at once, in the act that opens it, when the entry is open, on application by rule (its conditions, `holders fewer than n` and `passport from c`, hold together), sponsored, or after a conversion whose condition the applicant meets, the item closing admitted (the position held) or declined with the condition that failed; by an admission from the body that decides entry (by any one, or after a proposal passes), closing it admitted; by an admission from the office that assigns; or by a draw. A draw reveals a seed whose digest an earlier act committed for the position; among the applications opened before that commitment and open still, the one whose digest with the seed is least is admitted, and the others are declined as not drawn. An office assigns a party with or without an application, and where the exit is by the office, ends an assignment by `assign` naming the position. A party leaves every position at once by `leave` naming `all`, its commitments staying as they are.

**Definition 53 (question, ruling, precedent).** A question names what it is about (a norm, a rule, a holding, a term and the like, as its route admits) and a subject; its *subject* is the pair. The route's body answers it: ruled, with a reading, which enters the register of rulings and stands; referred, to a ruling in force on its subject; answered; declined. A question on a subject with a ruling in force is refused with the ruling cited, unless it asks the body that reviews rulings to review that ruling; such a review is answered by the review body alone, upheld, or ruled with the ruling it overrules named, which then stands superseded. An overruling comes from no one else and on no other question.

**Definition 54 (petition).** A petition names a kind, and the route for petitions of that kind says how it is met: by a deposit within a holding that declares review, the petition met when another origin's attestation checks the deposit that names it; by a decision (named, or the rule of amendment at a level), the petition adopted with the proposal that carries it under that decision when the amendment applies it, or by an answer of the decision's body once that proposal has passed; or carried by an office to another community. The route's body may decline it.

**Definition 55 (recusal, overdue).** No party to an item answers it or decides it: not its author, not the party that answers for it, not a party of the same origin. An item with a term is *overdue* after it, a mark the profile shows every reader and the briefing shows those who answer it.

**Invariant G8′ (a route for every directive).** A community that declares its routes declares one for applications, for questions and for petitions, and for every type of item its declared kinds open, each possibly empty; their bodies, decisions, holdings and offices are declared, and their restatement of the route for claims agrees with the part `claims`. Admission refuses a constitution that does not, under condition 4; G8 is the case of claims. A community that declares no routes is held to G8 alone, and every older record replays.

**Proposition 26 (the third version is a revision of the second).** The base's third version is admitted as a revision of the second, directly and by kategoria, renaming nothing: every kind keeps its name and register, the three declared directives stand beneath `request`, and every record of the second version, carried along it, loses nothing and is the left Kan extension of the record along the map of kinds.

*Proof.* The map of kinds is the inclusion of the second version's forest in the third's, which adds three kinds beneath `request` and changes no parent arrow, so it is a functor keeping registers and every fixed kind (kategoria's Proposition 9.4); each declared kind keeps the force of what it counts as (Definition 31). The carrying is kategoria's Proposition 9.10. ∎ The checks admit the revision both ways, carry 50 random records of the second version, and see an act of `apply` kept as written with its reading a typed request.

**Proposition 27 (every item reaches a body able to answer it).** Let a constitution declare its routes and be admitted. Then every item the fold admits either closes in the act that opens it (an application decided by rule, by sponsorship or after a conversion) or stands open on a route on which some party may answer it, being among those the route names and no party to it, unless the route's body has no holder; and its author's briefing shows it with its status.

*Proof.* An item opens only when its type has a route that is declared and not empty (Definition 51; G8′ by admission); an application under a body or an office has the decision's body or the office as its answerers; a question its route's body, or the review body for a review; a petition its decision's body, the custodian of its holding or its carrier. A party is excluded only by recusal, which removes the item's own author, bearer and their origin. The briefing lists every item whose author or bearer the party is. ∎ The checks open random applications, questions and petitions on 60 random chronicles (784 items) and find each either closed in its act, or open with some party able to answer it, and shown to its author.

**Proposition 28 (at most one ruling in force on a subject).** At every prefix of a chronicle, for every subject at most one ruling stands; a question on a subject with a ruling in force is admitted only as a review of that ruling; and a ruling is superseded only by an overruling from the body that reviews rulings, on a question that asked it to review that ruling.

*Proof.* A ruling enters the register only by an answer `ruled` to an open question. A question is admitted on a subject with a ruling in force only when it reviews that ruling; a ruled answer to a question on a subject with a ruling in force is admitted only when it names that ruling in `overrules`, which it may only on a review, from the review body, and which supersedes it in the same act. So each act that adds a standing ruling on a subject removes the one standing before it, by induction on the prefix. ∎ The checks run 100 random chronicles of questions, rulings and reviews (453 rulings, 156 overrulings) and find at most one ruling standing on each subject at every prefix, every question and answer admitted exactly as an independent reading of the routes says.

**Proposition 29 (applications decided by the boundary rule).** Under an entry on application by rule `holders fewer than n`, an application is admitted exactly when its author holds the position neither already nor by an open application, and closes admitted exactly when fewer than n hold it at its turn; the holders are then never more than n. A draw admits the application its seed's digest selects among those open before the seed was committed, and no other.

*Proof.* The fold decides the application in the act from the positions the record shows at that turn, and enters the position only when the condition holds; a holder leaves only by `leave`, which lowers the count. The draw's winner is a function of the revealed seed and the candidates, which the commitment fixes before the seed is shown; the admission names its party and is refused unless it is that winner. ∎ The checks run 100 random chronicles of applications and leavings under n from 1 to 4, each decided as an independent reading of the rule says and the holders never more than n, and 100 draws, each giving the position where the seed's digest says.

*Remark.* G1 (record): an item, its answer, an admission, a ruling and an overruling are acts of the chronicle; the register of rulings is computed from them. G2 (standing): `request` and the declared directives are directive and give no duty or power; a ruling binds as the community's declarative answer, an admission enters a position as before. G3 (answerability): each item records its author and the party that answers for it. G5 (separate registers): an application is never a position until decided, a question never a ruling until answered. G6 (rules as data): routes are a part of the constitution, amended as any part is, a petition's route among them. G7 (exit with the record): `leave` with `all` leaves every position and keeps every commitment owed. G8′: above. G9: items, rulings and positions change only by admitted acts.


## 15. Posted Work

In the first attempt at a society of agents, the tool that ran the writing of a book refused to let a section reviewed "revise" be taken again, and the steward had to ask the person whether the tool might change. Decision 0012 makes the life of posted work data of the community: a kind of work declares who posts it, who may take it and how, how long a taking runs, what its deliverable is and how it is checked, who reviews it with which verdicts, who accepts it, what follows a return, and what must come first; a refusal of the life is then a rule of the community, and changing it is an amendment decided by the body its constitution names.

**Definition 56 (kind of work, work item).** A constitution's part `work` declares kinds of work, each with the positions that post it; how it is taken (by the taker's own commitment naming the item, or by an office's taking of the taker's offer, an award); how many may take an item at once; who is eligible (holders of a position, and for a review another provider or another origin than the writer of the item reviewed); the term of a taking; its deliverable, within a holding, with a check and the office that enters the check's result; its review (none, by another origin, by another provider, or by a position), with its verdicts and those it accepts; who accepts it (an office, or a decision); the turns within which a returned delivery may be delivered again; and what must come first (kinds or items accepted). A request that names a kind posts a *work item*, with its specification, its matter, the item a review bears on, a due turn, and a reservation to named parties. The base taxonomy's fourth version makes items an effect primitive and their reopening an effect by law, the ground's own.

**Definition 57 (the life of a work item).** The state of an item at a turn is a function of its record and the turn. A taking is *taken* until something is delivered on it, and *lapses* at its term with nothing delivered. A delivery that the kind checks awaits its check (*delivered*) and, failing it, leaves the taking taken; a delivery that passes, or needs no check, is *delivered* until reviewed. A review with a verdict the kind accepts leaves it *reviewed*; any other verdict *returns* it, and the taker may deliver again within the declared turns, after which, or at once where the kind declares none, the taking *ends*. A taking accepted is *accepted*. An item is *withdrawn* by its poster, *accepted* when as many takings as its kind admits takers are accepted, *waiting* while what must come first is not accepted, *open* while fewer takings hold or are accepted than its takers, and otherwise in its taking's state. An item past its due turn and neither accepted nor withdrawn is *overdue*. The checks hold this function to an independent reading on 500 random records of takings, deliveries, checks and reviews.

**Definition 58 (the acts of the life).** A request naming a kind posts an item, by a holder of a position the kind names. A commitment naming the item (or an offer an office that posts it takes) takes it, only while it is open, by a party the kind and the item find eligible. A delivery on a taking is admitted while it is taken or returned within its redelivery, and refused when it lapsed, ended, or awaits its check, review or acceptance. The office the kind names enters the check's result. A review is admitted by a party the kind's review admits, never from the deliverer's origin, once on each delivery that passed its check, with a verdict of the kind's. An acceptance is admitted from the kind's office or decision, never from the taker's origin, and only after the check and a verdict the kind accepts. A poster withdraws an item no one holds. A release, cancellation or handing over of the taking's commitment ends the taking. The kind, not the choice rules, says who performs each of these acts, as an item's route says who answers it. A kind's life is amended as any rule is (`change.work`), and an item lives under its kind's life as it stood when the item was posted, so that an amendment governs the items posted after it and judges no past act anew (G6).

**Definition 59 (review of a holding by another provider).** A holding may declare review by another provider: what is deposited within it stands checked only when a party whose provider differs from its depositor's attests it. It may declare verdicts and those that check: an attestation with a verdict outside them records the review and leaves the deposit pending.

**Definition 60 (conditions on application that read posted work).** Entry on application by rule may ask that some posted work of a kind be open (`work open of kind k`), or that some open review be one the applicant's provider may give (`work open for the applicant's provider`): a position is then entered while there is work for it.

**Proposition 30 (exclusive taking).** At every prefix of a chronicle and every turn, no work item is held by more takings, active or accepted, than its kind admits takers.

*Proof.* A taking is created only by a commitment naming the item, or the award of an offer on it, each admitted only in the item's state open, that is when fewer takings hold or are accepted than its takers, and each adding one; a taking stops holding by lapse, end, release, cancellation or handing over, and an accepted one counts until the item is accepted. ∎ The checks run 200 random chronicles of postings, takings, deliveries, checks, reviews and acceptances, counting at every turn the takings that hold or are accepted, and find none above the kind's takers.

**Proposition 31 (adoption by another provider).** In a community whose kind of work declares review by another provider, every accepted item has, on the last delivery of its accepted taking, a review with a verdict its kind accepts by a party whose provider and origin differ from the deliverer's.

*Proof.* An acceptance is admitted only in the state reviewed, which needs a review with an accepted verdict on the last delivery that passed its check; a review is admitted only from a party whose origin differs from the deliverer's and, by the kind's declaration, whose provider differs too. ∎ The design calls this the adoption by another provider: what the first attempt's tool enforced by code, the community declares, and can change by its own procedure. The checks run the same 200 random chronicles, with reviews attempted by the writer's provider, by the writer's own principal and by others, and find every accepted item reviewed so.

**Proposition 32 (the fourth version is a revision of the third).** The base's fourth version is admitted as a revision of the third, directly and by kategoria, renaming nothing; every record of the third version carries to it as the left Kan extension.

*Proof.* As Proposition 26: the fourth version adds an effect primitive, a form of effect by law and fields read, and no kind, so the map of kinds is the identity of a forest onto itself. ∎ The checks admit the revision both ways and carry 50 random records.

*Remark.* G1 (record): every act of the life is an act of the chronicle, and the item's state a function of them and the turns. G2 (standing): a taking is a commitment, its duty to deliver commissive; an acceptance discharges it. G3 (answerability): a taker acting under a mandate is answered for by its principal, the commitment's debtor. G4 (guarded resources): what posted work draws on is charged along chains as before. G6 (rules as data): the life of a kind is data, amended as any rule is; the fault of the first attempt is the absence of a declared redelivery, cured by amendment, not by changing the tool. G9 (effects by act or by rule): a lapse and a reopening are effects by law, functions of the record and the turns, declared in the base.

## 16. Transmission, Counts and Compacts

Three things the design asks of a society whose members run on outside providers close the phases of thesmos ([decision 0013](../development/decisions/0013-transmission-counts-and-compacts.md)). An agent that reads a holding sends it to the model that reads it, so a community that cares where its holdings go must be able to say that a reading is a transmission. A community whose agents all answer to one person, or whose members are communities of unequal size, needs counts other than by origin. And communities bind one another by compacts, each by its own rule, as decision 0004 began and decision 0010 built for the smallest case.

**Definition 61 (transmission by operatives).** The base taxonomy's fifth version holds the category *reading by an operative*: an act of kind consult, use, lend or withdraw whose author is an operative with a declared provider other than `local`, read on the act's envelope and on what the registry of parties carries of its author, its kind and declared provider (Definition 27). Its counts-as rule makes such a reading count as a transmission to the author's provider (Definition 33), and applies only where a constitution declares, in its part `access`, that operatives transmit to their provider. The provider so reached is the request's second transmission beside the one its act declares: a permission decides the request only when it covers each, and a prohibition that names transmissions speaks to the request only when it names one of them. Open access, where no norm speaks, admits a reading whatever it reaches, as it admits any transmission (Definition 12).

**Definition 62 (counts by provider and by weight).** By provider, each provider weighs one, divided equally among the voters it provides: a voter's provider is its origin under provision (Definition 2), an operative's declared provider, and any other party itself, so that a person is a provider of judgement of her own. By weight, each origin under control weighs its declared weight w(o), a nonnegative rational, divided equally among its voters; an origin with no positive weight is not counted. The share in favour is Σ w(o) f(o) / Σ w(o) over the origins counted, f(o) the share of o's voters in favour.

**Definition 63 (thresholds and double majorities).** A threshold rule passes when the share in favour is at least, or more than, a fraction p/q ∈ [0, 1]. A double majority of members at least m and weight at least w passes when, over the origins counted by weight, the mean of f(o) reaches m and the weighted mean reaches w (either threshold may be strict). Every rule reads the ballots cast; a share of the whole body, which counts those who did not vote, would need a quorum, which the kernel does not yet declare.

**Proposition 33 (monotone rules).** Under every unit of count and every rule the kernel knows, turning a ballot from against to in favour never makes a passing decision fail.

*Proof.* Turning a voter of origin (or provider) o in favour raises f(o) and leaves every other term; every share is a nonnegative combination of the f(o), so it does not fall, and each rule is an upper set of the shares it reads (a double majority of two, consensus of the count against, which does not rise). ∎ The checks turn one ballot on 400 random electorates under every unit and rule and find no passing decision that fails.

**Proposition 34 (Proposition 6 under provision).** Under the count by provider: (a) every provider weighs one whatever the number of its agents; (b) when a provider whose agents vote alike runs any number more agents voting as they do, the share is unchanged; (c) when its agents divide, more agents move the share by at most 1/P, P the number of providers counted.

*Proof.* Proposition 6 word for word, with the criterion of provision for that of control. ∎ The checks run 300 random electorates, adding agents to each provider of models; a double majority is also held to a computation apart on 500 random electorates, and equal weights to the count by origin on 300.

**Definition 64 (compact).** A *compact* is an agreement among communities whose terms (`noetarium.compact/1`) declare its parties (or none, when any community may ratify it), its articles, each open or closed to reservation, whether it admits reservations, its rule of entry into force (all its parties, or a number n ≥ 2 of ratifications), its withdrawal (with a notice in turns, or none) and its last turn. Its digest is that of its canonical form, so that two communities ratify the same compact when their digests agree (after the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, 1969, Articles 2(1)(d), 19 to 24 and 54 to 56, read as the ground reads a treaty and no further).

**Definition 65 (ratification, reservation, withdrawal).** A community *ratifies* a compact by an amendment its own rule decides (`change.compact`, with its terms and reservations), refused when its terms do not stand, are another compact's, or name parties among which it is not, and while a ratification of it stands. A *reservation* names an article excluded from what binds the community, refused where the compact was ratified without its terms, admits none, lacks the article or closes it to reservation; it holds from its turn until the community withdraws it (`change.withdraw reservation`). A community *withdraws* by an amendment (`change.withdraw compact`), refused where the compact admits none; the withdrawal takes effect after the compact's notice, and until then the ratification stands. A ratification by name alone, as decision 0004's records made, admits no reservation and is withdrawn with no notice.

**Definition 66 (the standing of a compact).** Over the parties' records, the compact *entered into force* at the first turn at which its rule of entry held, that is at which every party named (or n parties) had a ratification standing. At a turn it is *in force* when it has entered, its last turn has not passed, and at least two parties' ratifications stand; a party is *bound* while its ratification stands and the compact is in force; an article *binds* two parties to each other when both are bound and neither reserves it, so that a reservation excludes its article between the reserving party and every other, both ways (Article 21(1)). Each party's ratification and withdrawal stand in its own record: a compact of several parties keeps them apart.

**Proposition 35 (each party apart).** For a compact, a party p and a turn t: whether another party's ratification stands at t does not depend on what p ratified or withdrew; and if the compact has entered by t, its last turn has not passed and at least two other parties' ratifications stand at t, every such party is bound at t whatever p does.

*Proof.* A ratification stands by its own turns alone. Entry depends on p only through the count of ratifications standing; with p's withdrawal removed that count is never smaller, so the entry turn is never later, and at t the count of others is at least two in both. ∎ The checks run 300 random records of two to five parties, ratifying, withdrawing and ratifying again, find the turn of entry equal to the first at which the rule held, turn by turn, and hold both parts at every turn when one party's withdrawals are removed.

**Proposition 36 (the disclosure agreement is the compact of its two sides).** A disclosure agreement (Definition 48) is in force at a turn exactly when the compact of two parties, the principal and the community, entering into force when both have ratified, with the agreement's notice and last turn, each side's ratification at its turn and the denouncing side's withdrawal at its denunciation, is in force.

*Proof.* The agreement stands from max(p, c) until min(to + 1, d + n). The compact enters at the first turn both ratifications stand, max(p, c) provided the denunciation has not taken effect by then, in which case neither is ever in force; it is in force while both stand, that is before d + n, and while the last turn has not passed; two parties being all it has, the count of two fails exactly when the withdrawal takes effect. ∎ The checks compare the two at every turn of 500 random agreements. The agreement remains in its own module, with its sides' own acts; the proposition says that compacts generalise it, ratification by amendment under each party's own rule and parties beyond two kept apart.

**Proposition 37 (the fifth version is a revision of the fourth).** The base's fifth version is admitted as a revision of the fourth, directly and by kategoria, renaming nothing; every record of the fourth version carries to it as the left Kan extension.

*Proof.* As Proposition 26: the fifth version adds two features, a category and a counts-as rule, and no kind, so the map of kinds is the identity of a forest onto itself. ∎ The checks admit the revision both ways and carry 50 random records.

**Proposition 38 (the option off changes nothing).** For a constitution that does not declare that operatives transmit to their provider, ratifies no compact with terms, reservations or withdrawal, and declares no weights, every act is admitted or refused as before decision 0013, and the relational digest is the same.

*Proof.* The provider reached is empty unless the declaration is made, so every request's transmissions, every condition met and every access recorded are as before; the digest writes what decision 0013 adds only when present. Every rule an older constitution states reads to the same rule, and a ratification by name keeps its old meaning (Definition 65). ∎ Every earlier record in the checks replays with its seals, and the check with the option off finds decision 0006's verdicts.

*Remark.* G6 (rules as data): whether readings are transmissions, how votes are counted and which compacts bind are all declared and amended as any rule is. G2 (standing) and G3 (answerability): a reading refused for its provider is refused before anything is sent; what an agent does with what it lawfully read is answered for by its principal, through the route for claims. A provider declared `local` is a declaration of the party, as its controller and provider are (decision 0009), and the community decides whether to accept it through its rules of presentation.


## 17. Orders, Terms and Seats

The design's Section 7.8 named four more parts a constitution may write ([decision 0014](../development/decisions/0014-orders-terms-and-seats.md)). The rule that places them: what has an effect in the fold is the ground's, and a label with no effect is data that the profile carries and registries read.

**Definition 67 (order, external data).** A constitution may declare its *order*, a label from an open vocabulary (a polity, an organisation, a cooperative, a foundation, a commons, a guild, a household, a treaty organisation, a league), and in its part `external` the registries it says it is listed in and its envoys, each a position whose holders are envoys or a record naming the community it is sent to, the holder and the position. The profile shows the order to every reader beside the community's name, and the external data wherever the part `external` is shown.

**Proposition 39 (the order and the external data are inert).** Two constitutions that differ only in their order and their external data have the same faults of admission, admit and refuse every act alike, and reach the same relational state with the same digest at every prefix.

*Proof.* Admission reads neither; the fold's step reads the constitution's positions, boundary, choice, bodies, decisions, scenes, conversions, claims, holdings, passports, routes, work, compacts and behaviour, and neither the order nor the registries nor the envoys; the relational digest writes neither. Induction on the chronicle. ∎ The checks run 100 random chronicles of 30 acts under the two constitutions, 680 of the 3,000 acts refused, and find every outcome and every digest equal.

**Definition 68 (term).** A position may declare a *term* n ≥ 1 in turns. It is read as the trigger rule "a position held for more than n turns ends" (Definition 34), an effect by law: at every settlement to a turn t, a holding of the position entered at turn e with t > e + n ends.

**Proposition 40 (a term is its trigger rule).** Let C_T declare terms n_p on positions p, and C_D be C_T without the terms, declaring in its part `behaviour` (extending the base) the trigger rules "a position p held for more than n_p turns ends". Then every act is admitted under C_T exactly when it is under C_D, with the same reason, and the two reach the same positions at every prefix.

*Proof.* The trigger rules in force under C_T are the base's with the terms' (term_triggers), those under C_D the base's with the declared ones, the same set of rules of one form; the taxonomy in force otherwise agrees, both extending the base. Settlement applies each rule of the form "end position" as a function of the positions and the turn, independently of the order of the rules, since each erases holdings of its own position only. The fold's other reading of the constitution is the same. Induction on the chronicle. ∎ The checks run 200 random chronicles of 30 acts (joins, leavings, admissions, applications), 832 positions ending by law, and find every outcome and every set of positions equal.

**Definition 69 (seats).** A body may declare its *seats* s ≥ 1. Its members at a state are the parties holding one of its positions. An act *fills the body beyond its seats* when, with the positions it would enter added, the body would have more than s members and more members than before. The fold refuses such an act (a founding, a join, an admission, a conversion); it refuses an amendment after which some body with seats would have more members than its seats; and an application decided by rule that would fill a body beyond its seats is admitted and closes declined, its finding naming the seats.

**Proposition 41 (a body never exceeds its seats).** At every prefix of a chronicle whose founding is admitted, every body with seats s has at most s members.

*Proof.* By induction on the admitted acts. The founding is refused when its first holders would fill a body beyond its seats. Positions are entered only by a founding, a join, an admission, a conversion and an application decided by rule (decision 0011), each refused or declined when it would add a member beyond the seats; a member already counted adds none. Positions end by leaving, by an office, by leaving every position and by a term; none adds a member. A body's seats or its positions change only by an amendment, refused when it would leave a body beyond its seats. A refused act changes nothing (invariant 5). ∎ The checks run 200 random chronicles of joins, admissions, applications, leavings and amendments of seats (2,042 joins, 500 refused for want of a seat, 565 amendments adopted) and find no body beyond its seats at any prefix, every join refused exactly when an independent count says the body would pass them.

**Proposition 42 (what is never reached changes nothing).** A constitution that declares no term and no seats holds no trigger rule beyond its taxonomy's and admits and refuses every act as before decision 0014, with the same relational digest; and seats and terms that a record never reaches change no verdict.

*Proof.* term_triggers is empty without terms; beyond_seats finds nothing without seats; the digest writes terms and seats only when present. When no body reaches its seats and no holding outlasts its term, every refusal for seats or end by a term is vacuous. ∎ The checks run 100 random chronicles under a constitution with seats and a term of a thousand and find every outcome and every set of positions equal to those of the constitution without them; every earlier record replays with its seals.

*Remark.* G2 (standing): a term ends a power by law and confers none; seats bound who may hold a power and confer none. G6 (rules as data): terms and seats are data, amended as any rule is (`change.term`, `change.seats`), and an amendment that would leave a body beyond its seats is refused, so that no position ends by it. G9: a term's effect is a trigger rule's over the record and its turns. The ground has no decision that ends a position, and no body that is a subset of another's members: a council elected from an assembly is a position of its own with a body of its own.
